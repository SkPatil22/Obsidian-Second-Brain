# Vannevar Bush

American engineer and science administrator (1890–1974). Published "As We May Think" in *The Atlantic* (July 1945) — the essay that described the [[Memex]] and planted the intellectual seed of personal knowledge management.

## Relevance to this vault

Bush's 1945 vision articulated the problem the [[LLM Wiki Pattern]] solves: a private, actively curated store of knowledge where the *connections* between documents are as valuable as the documents themselves — firmly on the wiki side of [[Wiki vs RAG]]. His open question — **who does the maintenance?** — went unsolved for 80 years. [[Andrej Karpathy]] frames the LLM as the answer (see [[Karpathy - LLM Wiki]]): the [[Compounding Knowledge Artifact]] is the Memex, finally realizable.

The vault is a direct realization of Bush's design goals:
- **[[Memex]]** → associative trails maintained by an LLM via [[Ingest Query Lint]]
- **[[Three-Layer Architecture]]** → raw sources · wiki layer · schema maps to Bush's external documents · maintained trails · maintenance rules
- **[[Compounding Knowledge Artifact]]** → the ever-growing store Bush imagined
- **[[Index and Log]]** → the navigation layer that keeps the trails findable as they grow
- **[[Obsidian]]** for browsing · Claude for maintenance · [[qmd]] for retrieval at scale — the modern tooling his vision called for

## Facts
- Directed the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) during WWII; organized large-scale scientific R&D.
- "As We May Think" (1945) described the Memex as a mechanized desk with microfilm-linked documents and *associative trails* — technology that would take decades to realize.
- Argued that the *connections* between ideas are as scientifically valuable as the ideas themselves — the philosophy this vault is built on.
